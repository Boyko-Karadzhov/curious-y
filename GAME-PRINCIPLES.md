# Curious-Y game principles

## Core inspiration

Curious-Y borrows the satisfying progression, anticipation, and spectacle of mobile games. Learning takes the place of spending money as the engine of progress. We want players to return because discovering something interesting feels rewarding and makes their game world grow.

Spend the player's attention on understanding. Make everything around learning effortless.

## Battle

Battle makes the player's progress visible and satisfying. It must not require tactical decisions, manual control, or close attention. Reserve the player's mental effort for learning.

Curious-Y is a classic tug-of-war game where two sides spawn units. The whole battle is automatic. Players should be able to recognize how their upgrades change the battle without studying its mechanics.

## Castle

The Castle is where players turn the rewards of learning into lasting progress by constructing buildings and purchasing upgrades.

Choices should be easy to understand and satisfying to make. Progression should not require calculations, research, or constant management.

## Rewards and chance

Use chance to create anticipation and the excitement of getting more than expected. Surprise rewards should make players look forward to the next discovery.

Every learning session should produce visible progress. Randomness can change the size or form of a reward, but an unlucky result should not erase the value of the player's effort. Players should understand what is guaranteed and what is left to chance.

## Learning

Learning should be easy to start, with short questions and clear language. Minimize unnecessary friction while preserving the thought needed to understand an idea. Multiple-choice questions are our main format.

Players do not need prior knowledge to participate. Guessing is welcome, and mistakes are opportunities to learn. Teach through questions and brief explanations: reveal why an answer is correct, not just which option was correct.

Players should progress through engaging with ideas, not merely tapping through questions. Give them opportunities to revisit or apply an insight so that understanding has value beyond the first answer.

More demanding exercises can be introduced when the reward makes the extra effort feel worthwhile, such as earning premium currency to speed up an upgrade.

We do not need to provide a complete learning course. Our aim is to spark curiosity through regular encounters with interesting ideas and encourage players to explore further themselves. Prioritize "Why" questions that reveal how or why something works over isolated trivia.

## Daily routine

Build around a short, satisfying daily routine that gives players a reason to return. Different days can reward different learning activities to add variety.

A session should have a clear point of completion: the player encountered an interesting idea and made visible progress. Continuing should be inviting, and stopping should feel satisfying. Missing a day should not undo earned progress.

## Everything else

Expand the experience when it makes learning more appealing, curiosity stronger, or earned progress more satisfying.

When engagement and learning conflict, protect meaningful learning. More playtime or more repeated actions alone are not enough to justify a feature. Success means players want to return, understand something new, and feel their time was well spent.
